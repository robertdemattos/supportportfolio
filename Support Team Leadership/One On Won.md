# One on Won's

The title should tell you why one on ones are so essential to support team leadership. Spending thirty minutes with each member of your team every week or every other week is a win for the leader and the support specialist alike. The best support teams are full of winners because they want to be the best at what they do and, most importantly, they want their customers to win too. This article provides a structure for running one on ones that actually work.

---

## What to Remember

The most important rule of the one on one is that this is the support specialist's time. Not the leader's. The leader runs the one on one to listen, learn, and advise. Think back to what was said about bosses. They make everything about themselves for their own benefit. Leaders show up to make sure their team benefits.

So how should a one on one be conducted? Some leaders take notes. Others prefer a flowing conversation. If a leader were to use a form, it should be divided into three ten minute segments. The first is about how the support specialist is doing as a person. The second covers any challenges they ran into during the week. The third is about what that person needs from their leader. Here is how each segment plays out.

---

## The First Ten Minutes: How Are YOU Doing?

The first segment is about warming the support specialist up in a genuine way. It also signals something important — that they are seen first as a person, not just a support agent with a ticket queue.

Questions to consider:

1. How are you feeling this week?
2. What did you do this weekend for fun?
3. If not fun, what did you do this weekend?
4. Or use an icebreaker tied to a shared interest — "Did you catch that game?" or "Are you going to see that movie? I heard it's good."

Write down briefly what they share. Over time a leader may find connections between something mentioned weeks ago and something coming up now. It also creates natural follow up questions that show you were actually listening.

---

## The Second Ten Minutes: Challenges, Questions, Concerns

This is where the leader listens for what is slowing the support specialist down or what they have run into that they have never seen before and need more help on.

Questions to consider:

1. Have you had any difficult issues to solve so far this week?
2. Would you like to schedule some time to look one over together?
3. Can we take a quick look at the problem right now?
4. Are you confident in the next steps to take?
5. Is there a ticket you would like a second pair of eyes on?

The only thing worth writing down in this segment is a reminder to schedule time with the support specialist if working through the issue together would take longer than the ten minutes available. Don't try to solve complex problems in the window. Just make the commitment and follow through on it.

---

## The Third Ten Minutes: What Do You Need From Me?

A good leader always checks in on whether they are meeting the needs of the people they lead. This segment is for exactly that.

Questions to consider:

1. Are your tools working the way they should?
2. Is there a report or process you don't fully understand?
3. Is there something I could be doing better for you in these one on ones?
4. Is there a process we could improve to make your day to day feel more manageable?
5. Do you just need to vent a frustration?
6. Would you like to know where you stand on your performance overall?

Write down whatever the support specialist brings up here. If there is something they want to see changed, that note deserves genuine follow up — either in a team meeting or at the next one on one. Dropping it is worse than never having asked.

---

## Final Thought

A good leader never forgets where they came from. There was a time when you were a brand new support specialist who knew little to nothing. If someone took the time to guide you and invest in your confidence, it made a difference. That is all a one on one really is — an investment in the person sitting across from you.

Sometimes one on ones shift in structure when a support specialist is struggling with performance. That is okay. The goal is the same. Help them get better. The format serves the person, not the other way around.

It will mean more than you know to the people you lead when you show up consistently and give them your full attention. One on ones are one of the simplest ways to show your team that they matter.
