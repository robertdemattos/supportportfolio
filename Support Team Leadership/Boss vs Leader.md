# Boss Versus Leader

You have had them before. You have had them since you started working. Someone to supervise you, make sure you stayed on task, and ensure your part got done. In any role there is generally someone calling the shots that you need to follow. If you were lucky, you got someone who treated you fairly, took their time to teach you what you needed to learn, and then stepped back to see what you could do on your own. If you were not so lucky, you were hired with them already forgetting your name and getting frustrated that you did not know something in week one. Or worse, you got someone who took every opportunity to step on you. In support, the person in charge sets the tone for how everything else goes.

---

## The Boss

The reason someone gets called a boss is because they are there solely to tell people what to do and exactly how to do it. Step one iota from the issued order and the person who received it is in for a bumpy ride. The boss rides that person constantly because they do not trust anyone to do the work without supervision, usually after bothering to show them once or not at all. A boss lets everyone else do the work while sitting around taking credit from the higher ups when something goes right. The boss goes home on time while the team works late under an unspoken "or else." The result is tired, overworked employees where half are already updating their resumes.

Here are some characteristics of a typical boss.

- Spreads toxicity throughout the team through the way they speak to people.
- Monitors constantly with the person being observed literally afraid to do anything for fear of being reprimanded.
- Overworks the team while making them feel their personal lives are secondary to company objectives.
- Openly embarrasses team members for bringing up ideas or solutions.
- Micromanages everyone, including telling people how to do things they already know how to do.
- Aligns with higher ups for personal career advancement rather than to advocate for the team.
- Generally a narcissist. Their way is the only way, even when it does not work in practice.
- Cancels time off at the last minute due to poor proactive planning.
- Keeps a revolving door of new employees because self-respecting people will not stay.
- Runs meetings where the team sits quietly while the boss talks, with no room for anyone else to get a word in.
- Creates disorganized service that costs the company in customer relationships and revenue.
- Makes the team's work about themselves rather than the people they are there to serve.
- Breeds more bosses by bad example.

Bosses are easy to spot. Walk into any business in complete disarray and you will find one at the source. In support, a boss can create enough disruption to put hundreds of thousands of dollars at risk. Think about it. You are a support tech working through a critical customer issue and your boss pulls you off it to handle something that benefits their standing with leadership or a favored colleague. Meanwhile the customer is quietly reconsidering who they pay for the product they need. Common sense is often the first casualty of a boss.

---

## The Leader

A leader contrasts a boss so sharply that the boss's behavior becomes impossible to miss. Companies can and will replace a boss with a leader once they see the difference. Leaders are humble, accountable, level headed, and willing to jump in wherever the team needs them. Their job is to make sure the team has everything it needs to succeed. Their job is to nurture the growth of every person they are responsible for. Leaders will throw themselves onto the tracks to protect their team from blame because they understand that when one person on the team has a problem, the team has a problem. Leaders are positive to a fault. There is no mountain of adversity the team cannot climb because that is the tone the leader sets.

Here are some characteristics of a true leader.

- Makes sure the team's cups runneth over in growth and success. Only once those cups are full does the leader's cup fill up.
- Kindness is hardwired into their character. There is no challenge that cannot be tackled when they put their heads together with the person they are helping.
- Celebrates wins and gives all the credit to the team.
- Learns from losses with the perspective that failure is acceptable as long as something was gained from it.
- Sets real, achievable goals so the team can improve steadily over time.
- Fosters a team that acts like true peers, helping each other without being asked.
- Protects the team's rest and manages external expectations so people can actually recharge.
- Runs meetings full of ideas and updates from everyone in the room.
- Builds camaraderie where humor and mutual respect are part of the daily culture.
- Gets their hands dirty alongside the team. If the team has a challenge, so does the leader.
- Coaches and mentors each person so everyone feels heard and represented.
- Says thank you. Every day.
- Builds teams that customers love working with, even when the issue is serious or difficult.
- Develops every team member into a leader in their own right because they were nurtured to be one.

Leaders are what companies actually want, even when they settle for bosses. In support the chain reaction from good leadership is clear. Happy, well rested, well mentored, positively encouraged team members carry that energy into every customer interaction. Customers stay even when the product is not perfect. Relationships are easier to manage because kindness and patience were there from the beginning.

---

## The Point

The tone set at the top determines whether money is made or money is lost. Bosses are dead weight. Leaders lift their teams up. The difference between the two is not subtle and the customer always feels it.
