# Using AI to Check Internal Communication Tone

One of the most underutilized applications of AI in a support environment is tone review for internal communication. Support agents work under pressure and sometimes the messages we send to colleagues land harder than we intend. A message written in frustration can damage a working relationship, create tension within the team, and make an already difficult situation worse.

This document walks through a real scenario, the kind that happens on every support team, and demonstrates how an agent could use AI as a checkpoint before sending a message that might do more harm than good.

---

## The Scenario

Abe was a support agent who struggled to build confidence in his role. He often escalated issues before exhausting his own troubleshooting steps and leaned on teammates more than the team felt was sustainable. Ashley, a more experienced agent on the team, reached her limit and sent him the following message in Slack.

---

## The Original Message

> Abe. All you seem to do is complain instead of taking a look at the actual issue. You need to slow down and look at the problem step by step. When I try to help you, you're just getting mad at me because you don't seem to understand. We can all look at your ticket and solve these issues. They aren't hard. You're making them hard for yourself and what's worse is when you don't understand them you pawn them off on others. We all have to pull our weight around here and you just aren't. You need to get it together or Carl is going to ax you. I also find that speaking to you makes me very upset. You need to find someone else to help you figure this out from now on.

---

## The Problem

Ashley's frustration was legitimate. The behavior she was describing was real and it was affecting the team. But the message she sent targeted Abe as a person rather than addressing the behavior. It referenced his job security, expressed her personal emotional state, and ended by cutting off the working relationship entirely. A message like this does not solve the problem. It creates new ones.

Before sending a message like this Ashley could have used an AI tool as a checkpoint.

---

## The Prompt Given to AI

*"I need to give feedback to a colleague who is struggling with their workload and leaning on others too much. I want to be direct and honest but I also want to be professional and not make it personal. Can you help me rewrite this message so it addresses the behavior without attacking the person? Here is what I originally wrote:*

*Abe. All you seem to do is complain instead of taking a look at the actual issue. You need to slow down and look at the problem step by step. When I try to help you, you're just getting mad at me because you don't seem to understand. We can all look at your ticket and solve these issues. They aren't hard. You're making them hard for yourself and what's worse is when you don't understand them you pawn them off on others. We all have to pull our weight around here and you just aren't. You need to get it together or Carl is going to ax you. I also find that speaking to you makes me very upset. You need to find someone else to help you figure this out from now on."*

---

## The AI Output

> Hey Abe. I want to be straightforward with you because I think it will help us work better together. When we work through tickets I've noticed that jumping to escalating or asking others for help sometimes happens before all the troubleshooting steps have been exhausted. I'd encourage you to slow down and work through the issue step by step before reaching out. I know support can be frustrating but working through the process independently builds confidence over time.
>
> I also want to be honest that our recent conversations have felt tense and I don't think that's good for either of us. I care about the team working well together which is why I'm bringing this up directly. If it would help I'm happy to walk through a ticket together so we can find a rhythm that works. But I do need our interactions to feel more collaborative going forward.

---

## Editorial Note

The AI version removes every personal attack. Gone are the references to Carl, to Ashley's emotional state, and to Abe being the problem. What remains is the core message — slow down, work through issues independently, and improve how we communicate. A colleague receiving the AI version might actually be able to hear it. The original version would only make someone defensive or defeated.

The agent's judgment here was recognizing that the original message was too personal before sending it and choosing to use AI as a checkpoint. That is the skill. AI did not replace the agent's voice or their legitimate concern. It helped them deliver that concern in a way that could actually create change rather than conflict.

Not every platform has this built in. Not every team has access to Slack's AI tier. But any agent with access to a generative AI tool has access to this kind of communication review. Knowing when to use it is what separates a professional from someone who just reacts.
