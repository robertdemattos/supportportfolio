# Using AI to Draft Responses for Difficult Customers

Some customer situations are genuinely hard to respond to not because the customer is wrong but because they are completely right. When a customer has been failed repeatedly, when the agent knows it, and when there is no immediate good news to offer, writing a professional and empathetic response in that moment is one of the hardest things a support agent has to do.

This document walks through one of those situations and demonstrates how an agent could use AI to help draft or refine a response when the emotional weight of the situation makes clear thinking difficult.

---

## The Scenario

A pharmacy customer had one of two point of sale units go down. The unit was booting to a message indicating it could not find an operating system. The support agent correctly identified the likely cause as a faulty SSD and coordinated with a hardware vendor to come on site.

The vendor replaced the system board instead of the drive without consulting the agent and left without calling to notify the agent that the work was done. Loading the operating system was the support agent's responsibility, not the vendor's. Without that call the agent had no opportunity to either send a drive with a preloaded image or remotely load the OS onto the repaired unit. The unit remained down. It took a week to get the vendor back out. The vendor then replaced the drive and left again without calling the agent. Once again the agent was never given the opportunity to load the operating system. The unit was still down with a blank drive and no OS. Nearly a month had passed.

The customer, Rhonda, was running a pharmacy with only two point of sale units. Being down to one line meant patients were waiting longer to receive their medication. She had been patient. Then she was not.

---

## The Customer's Email

> Your product is useless if I can't even use the point of sale it's supposed to be on. Your support is useless since you can't seem to get a fast solution like...oh...I don't know...maybe just REPLACING the POS and YOU dealing with the vendor issues. Of course...I shouldn't have to tell you how to do your damn jobs. We have been down to ONE LINE for patients for THREE WEEKS!! You can't even do your job well if you can't deal with the person who is fixing my POS which right now stands for PIECE OF S---!! How did you even get this job? I just gave you the solution to MY PROBLEM and did your job FOR YOU!! My patience is running out. Fix this NOW or lose a customer.

Rhonda was right. She should not have been put in the middle of vendor mismanagement and internal process failures. A pharmacy operating at half capacity for three weeks while waiting on a support team to coordinate a hardware fix is a patient care issue, not just a business inconvenience.

---

## Why This Is Hard to Respond To

The agent in this situation is also frustrated. They have been fighting with a vendor who is not listening, working within internal policies that prevent a simple unit replacement, and now absorbing a customer's fully justified anger. Writing a calm, professional, and empathetic response in that moment while also managing the active problem is genuinely difficult.

This is where an AI drafting tool can help. The agent describes the situation and their intent and asks AI to help structure a response that is empathetic, accountable, and professional without making excuses.

---

## The Prompt Given to AI

*"I need help drafting a response to a very upset customer. She runs a pharmacy and one of her two point of sale units has been down for nearly three weeks due to vendor mismanagement that was outside my control. She is rightfully furious. I want to acknowledge that she is right, apologize sincerely without making excuses, let her know I am escalating internally and working urgently to resolve this, and reassure her that she is valued. I do not have a resolution to offer yet but I want her to feel heard and to know that leadership is now aware. Can you help me draft this response?"*

---

## The AI Output

> Hi Rhonda,
>
> I want to start by saying that your frustration is completely valid and I am sorry. What you have experienced over the past three weeks is not the standard of support you deserve and I take responsibility for the overall handling of this situation even where the failures were outside my direct control.
>
> You should not have been put in the middle of this. A pharmacy operating at half capacity while waiting on a resolution affects your staff, your workflow, and most importantly your patients. That is unacceptable and I understand why your patience has run out.
>
> I have escalated this to my leadership team and they are now fully aware of everything that has transpired. We are treating this as the urgent priority it should have been from the beginning. I will be in contact with you as soon as I have a concrete update and I will not stop pushing until this is resolved.
>
> Thank you for continuing to communicate with us directly. I know that is not easy right now and I do not take it for granted.
>
> Sincerely,
> Rob

---

## The Agent's Actual Response

The agent had already drafted and sent a response before AI tools were widely available in the workplace. Here is what was sent.

> Hi Rhonda,
>
> Firstly let me apologize for the overall handling of this issue. You have every right to be upset and you should not have been put in the middle of mismanagement, process and policy. This may not mean much in this moment but please know that we very much value you as our customer and we realize that your priority is patients receiving their medication and the last thing you'd ever want to deal with is the aforementioned.
>
> Secondly I want to assure you that I will work double time with my team and the vendor to get this issue resolved for you. You are absolutely right. Three weeks is completely unacceptable. You're running a business and more importantly, you're part of the process of making patients feel better from what ails them by getting them their medication in a timely fashion. One line can stretch out the door. I've copied all of my leadership on this email and so they can see that I've communicated with you and please be rest assured that they are aware of all that has transpired and want to support me in giving you the great support you should have received to begin with.
>
> Thank you for being our valued customer,
>
> Sincerely,
> 
> Rob de Mattos
> 
> Tech Support Team

---

## Editorial Note

Both responses achieve the same goal. They validate Rhonda's frustration, take accountability without making excuses, and communicate that leadership is now involved. Neither response offers a resolution because there was none to offer yet. Both responses are honest.

The AI version is slightly tighter and leads with the apology more directly. The agent's version is warmer and more personal in places, particularly the acknowledgment of what a pharmacy means to patients. Neither is wrong. The value of the AI draft in this scenario is not that it produces a better response but that it gives the agent a structured starting point when the emotional weight of the situation makes it hard to know where to begin.

An agent who has just absorbed an email like Rhonda's may not be in the right headspace to write clearly. Having a draft to react to and edit is often easier than starting from a blank page. That is where AI earns its place in this workflow.

---

## What Happened After

The issue was eventually resolved nearly a month after the unit first went down, after the agent found a skilled vendor technician willing to work collaboratively to load the operating system correctly. Rhonda did not thank them. She expressed more frustration, which was understandable. A month of disruption to a pharmacy does not disappear the moment the hardware comes back online.

The lesson the agent took from this situation was not about the response. It was about the process. A customer should never be the last person to suggest the obvious solution. And a support agent should never be put in a position where internal policy prevents them from doing right by the customer they are trying to serve.
